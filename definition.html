What is FET?

Temperament
as Chemistry

FET proposes that who you are is shaped by three neurochemical systems — and that these systems can be mapped, measured, and understood.

Scroll to explore

What is
Temperament?

Temperament refers to neurochemically-based consistent individual differences in behavioural regulation in mentally healthy individuals. It is not about what you do, but the dynamics of how you do everything — your endurance, flexibility, tempo, and emotional dispositions.

Temperament is distinct from mood and personality. Mood is a temporary state — how you feel right now, shaped by immediate context or fatigue. Personality is a product of cultural factors — your values, motivations, and social identity. Temperament is the biological substrate beneath both: observable in animals and infants before culture has any influence, and remarkably stable across a lifetime.

The same neurochemical systems that underlie healthy temperament also underlie psychiatric disorders — as stronger imbalances of the same systems. This is the core insight of FET.

Temperament
Neurochemically based, stable across a lifetime, observable before cultural influence. Regulates the dynamics of behaviour — how fast, how long, in what direction.
Mood
A temporary emotional state. Fluctuates hour to hour based on context, sleep, and events. Distinct from the stable patterns that temperament describes.
Personality
Shaped by culture, upbringing, and experience. Reflects the content of behaviour — your values, beliefs, and social roles. Not what FET measures.

FET proposes that temperament can be understood through three independent neurochemical systems, each regulating a universal aspect of how behaviour is constructed.

NE
Orientation
Controls where attention goes — curiosity, novelty-seeking, and the drive to find patterns. Regulated by Noradrenaline (NE).
DA
Integration
Controls how quickly and flexibly you act — decisiveness, adaptability, and speed. Regulated by Dopamine (DA).
ACh
Maintenance
Controls how long you sustain effort — mental, social, or physical. Regulated by Serotonin (5-HT) and Acetylcholine (ACh).

Going Deeper

Beyond General Arousal
Previous models assumed a single arousal system explaining all individual differences. FET challenges this directly. Evidence from pharmacology, lesion studies, and clinical populations shows that Noradrenaline, Dopamine, and Serotonin / Acetylcholine independently regulate distinct functional aspects of behaviour. A drug raising Dopamine increases Motor Tempo without affecting Mental Endurance — regulated by Acetylcholine. The systems are genuinely separate.
Functional Constructivism
FET is grounded in the view that behaviour is generated anew each time — as a contingent interaction between available neurochemical resources and situational demands. Temperament is not a fixed entity but a dynamic ensemble. The same person may show different behavioural profiles in physical versus social versus mental activity — because the neurochemical systems regulating each are distinct.
Temperament and Psychopathology
FET treats mental illness and healthy temperament as weak and strong imbalances of the same neurochemical systems. Depression reflects disrupted serotonin–opioid interaction affecting endurance and integration. Generalised anxiety involves dysregulation of noradrenaline and kappa-opioid systems. This is directly testable — with implications for biomarker development and treatment selection.
Why Lexical Models Fall Short
Models built from factor analysis of language — Big Five, MBTI — confound biological and cultural traits. Agreeableness mixes Empathy (regulated by oxytocin and NE) with politeness (culturally determined). Linear statistical methods cannot capture the nonlinear, reciprocal dynamics between Dopamine and Serotonin. FET was built from neurophysiological evidence upward — not from language analysis downward.
Activity-Specific Approach
FET distinguishes physical-motor, social-verbal, and mental-analytic aspects of behaviour. A person may have high Mental Endurance (ACh-dominant) but low Social Endurance (5-HT-dependent). The same neurotransmitters operating in different activity contexts produce different trait expressions — specificity absent in all major competing models.
Measurement — STQ-77 & SEP-STQ
The Structure of Temperament Questionnaire (STQ-77) operationalises FET for empirical research. Validated across multiple samples and languages, it produces 12 temperament scores corresponding directly to the FET matrix. The SEP-STQ extends measurement to the personal environment individuals construct — testing whether temperament predicts environmental choices across cultures.
