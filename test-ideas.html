For Review — Dr. Irina Trofimova

Two ideas for
visualising the ensemble

Both proposals attempt to address the core challenge you raised: making it visually impossible to read FET as a 1:1 NT-to-trait mapping, while remaining accessible to a general audience.

The problem being addressed: Even with matrix layouts and colour coding, viewers tend to read "NE = Orientation" rather than understanding that each trait is regulated by a team of neurotransmitters — with one leading and others supporting. These two proposals attempt different solutions to that problem.
01
First Proposal

The Recipe Metaphor
— "FET Kitchen"

Each trait is presented as a recipe with a leading ingredient and supporting ones. Selecting a trait triggers an animation: individual neurotransmitter "bottles" appear above a bowl, pour their contents in proportion to their contribution, and mix together to produce the trait. The result makes it visually clear that no single bottle alone produces the dish.

The pour animation makes the ensemble process active and visible — not just labelled
Bottle proportions show relative contribution — NE does not dominate equally in all traits
Recipe metaphor is culturally universal and requires no scientific background
No animation on load — only triggered on click, minimising data usage

02
Second Proposal

The Ensemble Matrix
— Ingredient Cards

The FET 3×4 matrix, extended with a colour-coded ensemble bar in each cell showing the relative team composition. Clicking any trait opens an Ingredient Card — a structured breakdown of the neurochemical recipe using the same leading/supporting format. The bar makes it immediately visible that the same NT appears across multiple traits in different proportions.

Colour bars in each cell make cross-trait NT participation visible at a glance
4-colour horizontal system as per your coding: NE blue, DA orange, ACh/5-HT green, Opioid purple
Ingredient card on click — detailed breakdown without cluttering the main view
Light background, larger fonts — designed for readability by all audiences
