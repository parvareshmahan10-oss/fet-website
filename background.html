What is the FET?

The Functional Ensemble of Temperament (FET) is a neurochemical model of temperament. It describes temperament as stable, biologically based differences in how people construct their behaviour, and ties 12 traits to specific teams of neurotransmitter, hormonal and opioid systems.

3 columns
Functional aspects of action
Orienting to a situation, integrating (speed of) action, and sustaining it with energy.
3 rows
Activity-specific traits
Mental, social-verbal and physical activities rest on different systems, so each is measured separately.
Not 1 : 1
Neurochemical ensembles
No single chemical equals a trait. Overlapping, interdependent systems work as a team to produce each one.

Nine traits describe how behaviour is oriented, integrated and sustained. Three emotional amplifiers (Neuroticism, Impulsivity, Satisfaction) strengthen those three aspects. Mental disorders are seen as stronger forms of the same neurochemical imbalances, so health and disorder lie on one continuum.

The 12 Traits — FET Structure

Traits are organised across three functional aspects (columns) and three activity domains (rows), with a fourth row for emotional amplifiers. Both assessments — STQ-77 and SEP-STQ — measure the same 12 scales. Under each trait: the main neurochemical systems involved.

The 12 Traits in Detail

PRO
Sensitivity to Probabilities
Behavioural orientation to internally developed plans and preferences based on the ability for probabilistic thinking, the efficient extraction and processing of new knowledge related to implicit features of events.
PL
Plasticity
The ability to adapt quickly to changes in situations, to change the program of action, and to shift between different tasks.
ERI
Mental Endurance
The ability to stay focused on a task even while experiencing distractions.
EMP
Empathy
Behavioural orientation to other people’s emotional states and needs.
TMS
Social Tempo
The speed of spontaneous speech on well-known topics, ability to understand fast speech, the preferred tempo of conversations, reading and sorting of verbal material.
ERS
Social Endurance
The ability to sustain prolonged conversations, or verbal activities, such as reading, creative writing or improvised/spontaneous talking (i.e., without the use of a template).
SS
Sensation Seeking
Behavioural orientation to seek special physical sensations and pleasures, a tendency for sensation-seeking and risk-taking behaviour.
TMM
Motor Tempo
The preferred speed of physical work or manipulations with physical objects in well-defined activities.
ERM
Motor Endurance
The ability to sustain prolonged physical activity.
NEU
Neuroticism
Expectations of negative outcomes in situations of novelty or uncertainty.
IMP
Impulsivity
Premature initiation of actions, often inadequate to situations.
SLF
Satisfaction
Relaxing disposition towards current states of events, positive attitudes in planning, “approval bias” in judgments.

About the STQ-77

The Structure of Temperament Questionnaire — Compact (STQ-77) consists of 77 items: 72 items across 12 scales (6 per scale) and 5 validity items. Responses follow a 4-point Likert scale from "strongly disagree" to "strongly agree". The STQ-77 has been translated into 22 languages and validated across multiple cultures. Reliability coefficients fall in the range .70–.86.

About the SEP-STQ

The Specialized Extended Phenotype STQ examines the environment a person constructs around themselves — their extended phenotype — across six life domains. It tests whether temperament predicts the niche an individual builds, independently of culture. It uses the same 12 FET scales but probes expressed environments rather than self-reported dynamics.
