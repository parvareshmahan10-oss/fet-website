Scientific context for the STQ-77 and SEP-STQ assessments

What is the FET Model?

The structure of adult temperament — biologically based individual differences in behaviour — can be presented in line with the universal architecture of mammalian behaviour: that is, in line with the stages and functional aspects of action construction. The Functional Ensemble of Temperament (FET) model consists of 12 components reflecting the regulation of behaviour across four universal aspects: three dynamical aspects (energetic, integration-related, and orientation-related), a probabilistic dimension (easy/known vs. uncertain/complex tasks), an activity-specific dimension (physical vs. verbal-social vs. mental), and an emotional amplifier of these dynamical aspects. The model is a derivative of Luria's neuropsychological model of behavioural regulation and Rusalov's STQ model (Rusalov & Trofimova, 2007).

There is no one-to-one correspondence between a single neurochemical system and any feature of behavioural regulation. Neurotransmitter, hormonal, and opioid systems regulating temperament traits work in overlapping teams, with functional specialisations that reflect the functional aspects of action construction. These systems are strongly interdependent, contingent on each other's state, and have feedback relationships. For this reason the model is called a Functional Ensemble: temperament traits emerge from relationships between specific neurochemical systems, not from any single system acting alone. The FET covers neurotransmitters and neuromodulators including NE, DA, ACh, 5-HT, GABA, Glutamate, Oxytocin, Estrogen, Testosterone, Histamine, Adenosine, Adrenaline, Cortisol, opioid receptors (κ, μ, δ), Substance P, and other neuropeptides (PRL, GH, SOM, NPY).

The 12 Traits in Detail

PRO
Sensitivity to Probabilities
Tendency to notice and respond to uncertain, probabilistic, or ambiguous signals in the environment. High PRO = sensitive to novelty and unpredictability.
PL
Plasticity
Ability to efficiently switch between mental strategies and adapt to changing task demands. High PL = cognitive flexibility; Low PL = preference for routine.
ERI
Mental Endurance
Capacity to sustain intellectual effort over time. High ERI = able to work on complex problems for extended periods without fatigue.
EMP
Empathy
Orientation toward other people's emotional and mental states. High EMP = attuned to social signals; associated with oxytocin systems.
TMS
Social Tempo
Speed of social-verbal interaction — how fast one naturally engages in conversation and social processing. High TMS = fast-talking, quick social responses.
ERS
Social Endurance
Capacity to sustain social interaction without fatigue. High ERS = can engage in extended social activity; Low ERS = socially draining quickly.
SS
Sensation Seeking
Drive to seek novel physical experiences and sensory stimulation. High SS = drawn to physical adventure, risk-taking, and new sensory input.
TMM
Motor Tempo
Natural speed of physical movement and motor action. High TMM = fast physical reactions and movement; Low TMM = deliberate, slower-paced.
ERM
Motor Endurance
Capacity to sustain physical activity. High ERM = able to engage in prolonged physical effort; associated with serotonin and opioid systems.
NEU
Neuroticism
Emotional sensitivity to failure, threat, and negative outcomes. Amplifies negative affect. Associated with HPA axis and kappa-opioid systems.
IMP
Impulsivity
Tendency toward rapid, unplanned responses. High IMP = acts before thinking; linked to sympathetic nervous system and delta-opioid/dopamine interactions.
SLF
Dispositional Satisfaction
Baseline mood and sense of wellbeing. High SLF = dispositionally positive, content; associated with mu-opioid and serotonin systems.

About the STQ-77

The Structure of Temperament Questionnaire — Compact (STQ-77) consists of 77 items: 72 items across 12 scales (6 per scale) and 5 validity items. Responses follow a 4-point Likert scale from "strongly disagree" to "strongly agree". The STQ-77 has been translated into 22 languages and validated across multiple cultures. Reliability coefficients fall in the range .70–.86.

About the SEP-STQ

The Specialized Extended Phenotype STQ examines the environment a person constructs around themselves — their extended phenotype — across six life domains. It tests whether temperament predicts the niche an individual builds, independently of culture. It uses the same 12 FET scales but probes expressed environments rather than self-reported dynamics.
